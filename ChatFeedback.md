## How to read this report

- **Correction** means a definite typo, grammatical error, punctuation error, duplicated wording, or missing word.
- **RTL/typesetting** means Hebrew-English directionality, quotation-mark orientation, punctuation placement, or spacing should be corrected in layout.
- **Style** means an editor would probably revise it, but the author may choose to retain the oral voice.
- **Source/fact-check** means the wording should not be silently corrected until the cited source, name, date, quotation, halachic formulation, scientific claim, or historical claim is verified.
- **Sensitivity/tone** means the passage may read as demeaning, stereotyped, sensational, overly absolute, or insufficiently careful for publication.

## Book-wide editorial issues

1. **Create and enforce a transliteration/style sheet.** The manuscript varies among forms such as `Sefer/sefer`, `parsha/parasha`, `Chayai/Chaya Sara`, `Maasei/Massei`, `Hoshana Raba/Rabba`, `Mussaf/Musaf`, `Tamuz/Tammuz`, `haTorah/ha’Torah`, and several spellings of rabbinic names and titles. Decide on one system and apply it to headings, body text, captions, URLs, and index entries.
2. **Standardize capitalization.** Torah, Reform, Allied, Sivan, Hashem-related pronouns, names of sefarim, and holiday names are inconsistently capitalized.
3. **Standardize quotation style.** Dialogue punctuation is frequently placed outside the closing quotation mark, outer and inner quotations are reversed, quotation marks remain unclosed across paragraphs, and smart quotes face the wrong direction near Hebrew. Use a consistent hierarchy, preferably double quotation marks for the main quotation and single quotation marks for quotations within it.
4. **Perform an RTL/LTR layout pass.** Mixed Hebrew-English lines need directional isolation so that Hebrew phrases, parentheses, punctuation, dashes, footnote markers, and quotation marks do not migrate to the wrong side. This is especially important on pp. 241–248, 341–382, 423–430, and throughout pp. 439–458.
5. **Replace spaced hyphens used as dashes.** Many passages use `word - word`; use an em dash or recast the sentence. Retain true hyphens in compounds such as `long-time`, `Torah-true`, `next-to-last`, `two-second`, `old-age`, and `trickle-down`.
6. **Line-edit oral transcription.** Numerous pieces retain false starts, repeated setup phrases, rhetorical questions, sentence fragments, filler such as “so,” “now,” “yeah,” “boy,” and “by the way,” and long comma splices. This is most pronounced in the long Shabbos piece, pp. 439–458, but appears throughout.
7. **Standardize the brand name `TorahAnytime`.** The manuscript and index use `Torah Anytimes` and related variants.
8. **Check every footer URL from the source file rather than OCR.** Confirmed or strongly visible problems include `/purin/` on pp. 426 and 428 and `hitps://` on p. 437. A final automated hyperlink check is recommended.
9. **Rebuild the index after copyediting.** Names, spellings, titles, and pagination may change. Run a concordance check so every entry points to the intended page and every recurring source uses one spelling.
10. **Distinguish teaching, interpretation, and fact.** Many stories and claims are presented as established history, science, medicine, or halachah without a source. Attribute traditional stories explicitly and soften absolute formulations when the source supports only an interpretation.
