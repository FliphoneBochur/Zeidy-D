Scope: full-document text-layer scan of the 474-page PDF, with rendered-page verification where Hebrew/English directionality or punctuation could be misleading. The report distinguishes definite corrections from consistency/style recommendations. Hebrew/English bidi order was not flagged merely because text extraction reversed it; only visible/actionable issues are included.

## Global consistency recommendations

- **Quotation punctuation:** The sefer mostly follows American English conventions, but there are scattered instances where commas/periods are placed outside closing quotation marks, spaces appear before closing quotes, or commas before dialogue tags are omitted. The report below lists many concrete occurrences; the whole document should be normalized to one convention.
- **R’ / R':** Both curly and straight apostrophes occur. Standardize to **R’** (or whatever house style you choose).
- **parsha / parshah:** Both spellings occur. Pick one house spelling and use it throughout.
- **bezras / b’ezras:** Both forms occur. Pick one transliteration.
- **medrash / midrash:** Both forms occur. Pick one transliteration.
- **ArtScroll:** Where the publishing brand is intended, use the official capitalization **ArtScroll**, rather than “Artscroll.”
- **Dash style:** Hyphens are frequently being used as parenthetical dashes. For a polished book, standardize hyphen/en dash/em dash usage.
- **Hebrew-English transitions:** In the rendered pages checked across the book, the RTL/LTR handoff is generally functioning correctly. Many apparent reversals in extracted text are PDF bidi artifacts, not print errors. The recurring real problems are punctuation adjacent to Hebrew words—especially question marks and quotation marks—not wholesale reversal of Hebrew phrases.
